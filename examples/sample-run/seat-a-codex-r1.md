## ATTACK

1. **“If a wrong answer costs more than ~15 minutes, panel” is fake precision.** No basis is given for 15 minutes, and expected loss—not raw consequence—is the relevant quantity: probability of model error × impact × panel effectiveness. This rule would panel routine reversible work while potentially skipping rare but catastrophic errors that initially look cheap. Use explicit risk factors: irreversibility, blast radius, ambiguity, observability, and verification cost.

2. **Attributed rebuttals invite exactly the bias the design claims to suppress.** Telling seats whether a claim came from Codex or Grok encourages brand stereotypes and authority discounting. “Known biases” such as “Grok over-indexes on punch” are unsupported generalizations, not calibrated reliability estimates. Claims should be blind during adjudication wherever feasible; attribution belongs in the audit record, revealed only when provenance materially affects evidence quality.

3. **The orchestrator’s authority is internally contradictory.** It is supposedly “a switchboard, not a chair,” yet it verifies citations, selects disputed claims, decides what gets dropped, assigns buckets, and issues the ship/don’t-ship call. Those are substantive adjudication powers. Mechanical templates do not remove framing bias. The methodology needs an explicit separation between neutral relay, empirical verification, and final decision authority—with logged transformations and human escalation rules.

## CONCEDE

- **Independent first-round findings must remain isolated.** Agreement before cross-talk is materially stronger evidence than agreement produced after anchoring and tone-matching.
- **Verdicts must preserve unresolved dissent and identify the cheapest discriminating test.** A forced consensus destroys information precisely where the panel is most useful.

## REVISE

My round-1 position is not included here, so I cannot honestly claim a specific revision. The closest substantive update is this: I would strengthen any prior preference for free-form adversarial exchange into **claim-addressed exchange with stable IDs and explicit dispositions**. That improves traceability without accepting the other panelist’s overly rigid “relay only disputed claims” rule; dependencies and omitted assumptions may also need relay.

## VERDICT INPUT

1. Round 0 must be independent, parallel, and based on the same frozen brief and evidence access.
2. Every material claim must have a stable ID, evidence, confidence/severity, and a falsifier or discriminating test.
3. Relays must preserve claim meaning, log all summaries or omissions, and avoid source attribution until needed.
4. The final verdict must separate independent agreement, empirically resolved disputes, and surviving dissent.
5. Stop based on marginal information value and decision risk—not consensus, arbitrary round counts, or fixed time thresholds.
