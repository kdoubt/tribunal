1. ATTACK: The opposing YES answers a narrower question than the brief. The proposal is “raise isolation to REPEATABLE READ,” not “wrap SELECT+check+UPDATE in one Postgres transaction and treat 40001 as failure with full retry.” Those assumptions are not in the brief and are doing all the work. InnoDB RR still snapshot-reads the SELECT and current-reads the UPDATE: both sessions can pass `stock=1` and the second commit writes `-1`. Even on Postgres, aborting the UPDATE is not oversell-safety: autocommit (SELECT and UPDATE not one txn) never raises 40001; swallowing 40001 or retrying only the UPDATE still books two sales; recording the order before the UPDATE commits still sells the last unit twice with `stock=0`. Their own second claim (isolation bump is insufficient without 40001 policy) falsifies their first claim’s YES. Preferring a conditional UPDATE while calling the isolation change YES is the same false sense of safety the ticket is buying.

2. CONCEDE: On PostgreSQL, if both statements share one REPEATABLE READ transaction, the loser of a concurrent update is aborted with 40001 rather than applying `stock-1` to a newer version. Postgres does not auto-retry; the whole check-then-act must be retried or the attempt must fail. RR is not a portable SQL-standard guarantee. The conditional `UPDATE … AND stock > 0` (check `rowcount`) under READ COMMITTED is the better fix.

3. REVISE: No revision of the core claim. Switching isolation to REPEATABLE READ does not make this check-then-act oversell-safe (confidence 0.9). Narrow: Postgres + one wrapping txn + 40001-as-failure prevents `stock=-1` via abort, not via a correct reserve (confidence 0.85). Conditional UPDATE remains the cheap correct fix (0.95).

4. VERDICT INPUT: NO — do not ship RR as the oversell fix; biggest risk is two successful checkouts of the last unit (`stock < 0` or two orders). Cheapest test: two concurrent checkouts on `stock=1` (MySQL and Postgres); assert one success, `stock=0`, and 40001 never counted as a sale.
